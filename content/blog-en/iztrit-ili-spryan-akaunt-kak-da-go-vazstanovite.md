Short answer: a suspended account is blocked by the platform, but its data still exists and the decision can be appealed within a set deadline. A deleted account is gone for good and cannot be restored. So the first task with any suspension notice is working out which of the two situations you are in and how much time you have. The [sanctions reference](/vidove-sanktsii) puts both states side by side, with the places where each is visible.

These terms get mixed up constantly, and the differences decide what you can do at all. Let us sort them out.

## Deactivated, suspended, deleted: what each one means

**Deactivated by you.** You switched the account off temporarily. The profile is hidden, but everything is kept and comes back at your next login. There is no case here.

**Suspended or disabled by the platform.** The notice usually reads "Your account has been disabled" or "suspended" (on X and TikTok). You cannot log in, the profile is invisible, but the data sits on the servers. This state has a way out: an appeal. Facebook, for instance, states explicitly that you can appeal a disabled account within 180 days of the decision. On TikTok the appeal button appears when you try to log in and is also time-limited, so do not put it off.

**Deleted.** Once the appeal deadlines pass, after severe violations, or after long inactivity following a suspension, the platform removes the data permanently. There is no way back from there — for anyone, at any price. Whoever promises to restore a genuinely deleted account is lying to you.

The borderline case: the notice says "disabled", but when you try to appeal, the form answers that the account does not exist. That usually means deletion is already done or under way. Which is exactly why deadlines are the most important part of this article.

## Why accounts get suspended

In our practice the causes group like this, ordered by frequency:

1. **A Community Standards violation.** Real or apparent: content about restricted goods, nudity, harassment, copyright. Sometimes the violation sits in an old post from years ago, caught by a new sweep.
2. **"Suspicious activity".** Logins from unusual devices and countries, abrupt changes in behaviour, automation through banned tools. The algorithm does not explain; it just acts.
3. **Mass reports.** An organized group of users reports the account at once. The system sometimes suspends the profile pre-emptively, before a human has looked at the reports. We have seen it in business rivalries and with public figures.
4. **Doubt about identity or age.** A document or a video selfie is requested for confirmation. Missing the deadline leads to suspension.
5. **A link to another penalized account.** A shared device, email or ad profile tied to an already banned account drags yours down with it.

An important note: the platform is not obliged to be right. A significant share of the cases we take end in recovery precisely because the original decision was an automated error. But the error does not fix itself — it gets fixed through an appeal.

## What to do in the first 48 hours

**Save everything.** A screenshot of the notice, the emails from the platform, the exact wording of the violation. In an appeal, and later in a DSA complaint, those details are your evidence.

**Do not create a new account.** The temptation is strong, but building a replacement profile while the old one is suspended counts as ban evasion and is grounds on its own to lose both accounts. Wait for the appeal to resolve.

**Check your email, spam folder included.** Platforms often send a verification link or a document request that goes unnoticed. A missed reply deadline closes the case.

**Open the appeal form from the notice, not from a search engine.** Google results for "appeal form" are littered with phishing copies. Use only the link in the notice itself or the addresses in the official help centre.

## Which evidence carries weight in an appeal

Collect it before you need it, because after a suspension some of it becomes unreachable:

- **The suspension notice** with the exact wording and date. A screenshot, not a paraphrase.
- **Emails from the platform**, including old ones about logins from new devices; they prove when the account was under your control.
- **Proof of identity or of the connection to the brand**: an ID, a company registration, a domain with the same name, advertising invoices.
- **Context for the disputed content**: if a post was removed by mistake, the original file with its creation date and an explanation of the context both help.
- **The account's history**: when it was created, how it grew, previously resolved reports. These are recoverable from old emails and from your data archive, if you downloaded it in time.

Downloading that archive deserves its own sentence: both Meta and TikTok let you export all your content and data. Do it every few months. With a lost account, the archive is the difference between "I lost everything" and "I lost only the profile".

## If the account is a business one: a few specifics

A suspended personal profile that administers a business portfolio drags pages, ad accounts and a whole team's access down with it. That is why the rules are stricter for a business presence: always at least two portfolio admins, a clean separation of personal and work, and confirmed business verification in Business Manager while everything still works. A verified business has wider appeal channels, and business support agents can see the case as a whole.

If you lost the personal profile and access to business assets at the same time, the order of recovery matters: the personal account first, then the access rights. The other direction usually hits a wall, because business channels require a working admin profile.

## How to write an appeal that works

An appeal is not a plea for mercy but a short objection on the merits. The structure we use:

1. Who you are: username, email, how long the account has existed.
2. Which decision you are appealing, with the date of the notice.
3. Why it is wrong: either you did not commit the stated violation, or the context was misread. One or two specific sentences.
4. What you are attaching: evidence, documents, references.

Keep the tone neutral. Sentences like "I make my living from this account" are understandable, but they do not move the decision; the classifier is looking for facts about the violation. If the platform asks for a video selfie to confirm identity, record it in good light and without filters; a rejected verification adds days to everything.

One more thing that saves headaches: appeal once and wait. Five identical appeals in three days do not speed up the queue, and in some systems they reset your place in it.

## If the appeal is rejected

The first rejection is not always the end. The directions that work after it:

- **An internal appeal at a higher level.** In the EU, the Digital Services Act obliges platforms to run an internal complaints system for at least six months after the decision.
- **An out-of-court body under the DSA.** The Appeals Centre Europe handles disputes with Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube independently of the platform. How those channels work is covered in detail in the article on [reaching support](/blog/svarzvane-s-poddrazhkata-na-instagram-facebook-tiktok).
- **A professionally prepared case.** When an account carries commercial value, a well-structured evidence file changes the odds. That is the work we [do every day](/services): ordering the facts, writing the objection and following the case as it moves.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does an appeal for a suspended account take?

Usually two days to four weeks. Additional verifications (a document, a video selfie) add a few more days. No response within a month is a signal to escalate rather than to wait passively.

### Will I lose my followers and content?

With a successful appeal, the account returns to its state before the suspension: followers, posts and messages are all there. Loss only happens with permanent deletion.

### Can I just make a new profile?

Only once the old case is finally closed, and without copying the name and branding one to one while an appeal is running. Created earlier, a new profile risks automatic suspension for ban evasion.

### I do not remember the email and phone on the suspended account. Is it worth trying?

It is harder, but not impossible. The access recovery forms allow identification through a document and through old account details. The more specifics you can prove — creation date, previous usernames, linked devices — the more realistic the chance.
