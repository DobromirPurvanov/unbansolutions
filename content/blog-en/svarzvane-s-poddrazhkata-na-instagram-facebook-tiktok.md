Short answer: Instagram, Facebook and TikTok have no phone line and no public email for ordinary users. Contact runs through the forms in their help centres, through "Report a problem" inside the apps, and for businesses and Meta Verified subscribers there is a chat with a real person. Anything else promising you "direct contact with Meta" is usually a scam.

That is unwelcome news for anyone whose account has just been suspended. But the channels, few as they are, do work. The question is knowing which one fits your case and how to use it without hurting your position. If you are not yet sure which measure you are facing, start with the [diagnostic](/diagnostika) — it also shows where the status is officially visible.

## Why finding a human is nearly impossible

Meta serves more than three billion accounts and TikTok is well past a billion. At that scale, support is built around automation: algorithms make the first decision and humans step in only at the appeal stage, and not always then. Sending a fifth and sixth message through the same form rarely changes anything. What changes the outcome is which channel you enter through and what exactly you write.

There is a second reason, discussed less often. A public email or phone number would be buried in spam and fraud within hours. So platforms deliberately hide their direct contacts — and that empty space gets filled by fake "support agents" who message you first in comments and DMs. A rule with no exceptions: real support never contacts you first.

## Instagram's channels

For most problems the path is inside the app: Settings → Help → Report a problem. Reports sent from there arrive with your account details attached, which matters for tracking them.

Specific situations have their own entrances:

1. **Disabled account** — the "My Instagram account was disabled" form in the Help Centre (help.instagram.com). It works even when you cannot log in.
2. **Hacked account** — [instagram.com/hacked](https://www.instagram.com/hacked/). This is the dedicated flow for stolen profiles, including when the email and phone number have already been changed.
3. **Visibility problems** — these are not reported but checked: Settings → Account Status shows whether your content is eligible to be recommended.

If the account belongs to a business portfolio, go through Meta Business Suite. Business channels are handled separately and, in our experience, faster.

## Facebook and Meta Business channels

Personal profiles use the Help Centre (facebook.com/help) and the appeal forms that appear inside the restriction notice itself. For a hacked profile, the entrance is [facebook.com/hacked](https://www.facebook.com/hacked).

Advertisers have a wider door: the Meta Business Help Centre offers live chat when you have an active ad account. In our practice this is the fastest free way to reach a human at Meta, sometimes within minutes. A business support agent can also escalate problems with the personal profile behind the ad account, although they are not obliged to.

The third option is Meta Verified. The subscription includes access to support with real agents for Instagram and Facebook. It is not a guarantee of a solution — the agent works from the same rules — but it removes the hardest part: waiting in a void. For accounts a business depends on, the monthly fee often pays for itself through that access alone.

## TikTok's channels

TikTok accepts reports through Settings → Report a problem in the app and through the web feedback form (tiktok.com/legal/report/feedback). If the account is banned, an appeal button appears when you try to log in; that is the main path and it is worth using as soon as possible, while the option is still active.

Business accounts with running campaigns have TikTok Ads support with separate channels, again more responsive than the consumer ones.

## What about YouTube, X and LinkedIn?

We get asked often, so here they are in brief.

**YouTube** splits users in two. Channels in the Partner Programme (monetized ones) have Creator Support with chat and email straight from YouTube Studio → Help. Everyone else goes through the help centre and the forms for appealing strikes. The fastest reaction comes from @TeamYouTube on X: a public post describing the problem often gets an answer within hours.

**X (Twitter)** is the hardest case right now. The forms at help.x.com work, but human support for free accounts is effectively down to zero. X Premium subscribers get a priority queue, which is almost the only way to reach a person.

**LinkedIn** is the opposite example: the help centre offers chat with real agents, and Premium subscribers get priority. Restricted-account problems are resolved noticeably faster there than at Meta.

## How many attempts before you change approach

A working rule from our practice: one channel, two attempts, then switch. If the appeal form has stayed silent twice over two weeks, sending the same text a third time through the same form costs you a month. A sensible sequence looks like this: the in-app form, then the business channels (if you have access), then Meta Verified or its paid equivalent, and finally the DSA mechanisms below. Every level adds weight and a paper trail that the next level can see.

## How to write a request that gets an answer

The forms give you one text field and no context. Use it with discipline:

1. Identify the account precisely: username, email, phone number.
2. Describe what happened, with dates. "On 14 March I received a suspension notice" carries more weight than "you blocked me recently".
3. Name the specific rule you are said to have broken and why you believe the decision is wrong. One sentence on the merits, without emotion.
4. Attach evidence if the form allows it: screenshots of the notice, invoices, an ID document if requested.
5. Keep it short. Three paragraphs get read; three pages do not.

Anger, threats of lawyers in the first message, and templates copied from forums all make an answer harder. On the other side, a person or a classifier has to understand in seconds who you are and what you want.

## Your rights in the EU: when the platform stays silent

Since February 2024 the Digital Services Act (DSA) has given users in the EU real leverage. The platform must give you a reasoned decision for every restriction: which rule you broke and how the decision was made. It must also run an internal complaints system for at least six months after the decision.

If the internal appeal leads nowhere, you have the right to turn to a certified out-of-court dispute settlement body. For Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube that body is the Appeals Centre Europe in Dublin, which accepts complaints online and rules independently of the platform. Its decisions are not binding on the platform, but in practice they are taken seriously, because non-compliance is reported to regulators.

This is the slow route — weeks instead of days. But it is official, documented, and does not depend on an algorithm's mood.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Instagram or Facebook have a support phone number?

No. The numbers that surface in search results as "Instagram phone number" do not belong to Meta, and many of them belong to scammers who later ask for money or access codes. The only chat with a real employee runs through Meta Verified or through business support for advertisers.

### How long does a support reply take?

Meta's business chat: minutes to hours. Appeal forms: from 24 hours to several weeks, depending on the type of problem and the queue. If nothing moves within two weeks, it makes sense to resubmit with better-structured content or to escalate through a DSA channel.

### Can I write in Bulgarian?

Yes. The forms accept any language, and Meta and TikTok handle Bulgarian without trouble. English speeds escalations up slightly because it skips machine translation, but it is not required.

### Someone messaged me saying they are from Instagram support. Is that real?

No. Platforms do not contact users first through direct messages, comments or WhatsApp. Every such message, however official it looks, is an attempted scam. Do not open the links and do not share codes.
