Short answer: a restriction is a partial limit. Your account works and stays visible, but the platform temporarily switches off specific features: likes, following, comments, messages, live or advertising. Most restrictions are automatic, expire on their own within 24 hours to two weeks, and get extended if you keep up the same behaviour while they are running.

That is the good news here: unlike a suspension, a restriction rarely threatens the account. The bad news is that accumulating restrictions is the warning stage before heavier measures. So it is worth working out which restriction you caught and why; if you are not sure, run through the [symptom diagnostic](/diagnostika) or the [sanctions reference](/vidove-sanktsii).

## The six types of restrictions

**1. Action blocks.** The most common one: "Try again later" when you attempt to like, follow or comment. Instagram applies it after too many actions in a short window. It lasts from hours to a few days, and up to two weeks on repeat.

**2. Limited messaging.** A cap on new conversations with people who do not follow you. Typical after spam reports in DMs or mass identical messages.

**3. Reduced reach on specific posts.** A notice along the lines of "this post is not shown to some people". The targeted version of limited visibility; if reach has collapsed across the whole account with no notice, read our article on [shadowbans](/blog/shadowban-kakvo-e-kak-da-go-razpoznaete-i-premahnete) instead — the logic there is different.

**4. Suspended creator features.** A ban on live, on monetization or on adding links. It usually follows community rule violations or a low account "reputation" on TikTok.

**5. Advertising restrictions.** A separate universe: a restricted ad account, rejected ads, advertising switched off for an entire page or business portfolio. Here the fixes run through the Business Help Centre and the ad account review, not the personal profile. Which wordings and landing pages bring ads down is collected in the [advertising rules](/pravila/reklami).

**6. Hashtag and link limits.** Posts carrying a certain hashtag or domain stop being distributed. This often hits shortened links and domains reported as spam by other users.

## Where restrictions come from

The enforcement logic is predictable once you look at it from the platform's side. It defends the system against three things: automation, spam and risk.

- **Action pace.** Hundreds of likes or follows in an hour is a bot's pattern, even when a human does it by hand. New accounts have far lower thresholds than old ones.
- **Automation tools.** Mass-following apps, scheduling through unauthorized services, "account growth" on subscription. The platform sees their access to your profile.
- **User reports.** Enough reports in a short window trigger an automatic limit before any human review. We have seen campaigns by competitors that rely on exactly this.
- **Device and network overlaps.** Logging in from a device or IP address linked to penalized accounts pulls restrictions in "by proximity". Public VPN addresses are a classic source of such overlaps.
- **Grey-zone content.** For ad accounts: cosmetic procedures with promised results, financial offers, crypto. Rejected ads pile up as points against the account.

## How a restriction gets lifted

The first rule sounds obvious and is the most broken: stop the action that triggered it, for the entire duration of the limit. Every "let me check if it's over" attempt counts as a new violation and resets the counter.

The rest of the steps, in order:

1. **Check your notifications and Account Status.** They state the type of limit and how long it runs. If there is a "Tell us" or "Appeal" button, use it once, with a short and specific objection.
2. **Cut off the automation.** Open the security settings, review connected apps and active sessions, delete anything suspicious and change the password. Without this step, restrictions come back in cycles.
3. **Clear the trigger.** Remove the posts or messages reported as spam, and the hashtags from the affected posts.
4. **For advertising limits: request a review through the Business Help Centre.** The review of a restricted ad account is a formal process with human involvement; prepare business documents in case they are requested. With active campaigns you also have access to agent chat.
5. **Document it if the limit is clearly wrong.** Screenshots with dates are the foundation if you later escalate through an internal appeal or through the out-of-court bodies under the DSA, which we covered in the article on [suspended accounts](/blog/iztrit-ili-spryan-akaunt-kak-da-go-vazstanovite).

## Which tools are safe

The question comes up in every second restriction case, so let us separate it out. Safe tools are the ones working through official channels: Meta Business Suite and the approved partner scheduling platforms (they appear in Meta's integrations list), TikTok's own tools for business accounts, schedulers with official API access. What they have in common: they ask you to log in through the platform's official OAuth window and never ask for your password directly.

The dangerous category has three tells: it asks for your password in its own form, it promises actions the platform does not offer (mass following, "guaranteed growth", unfollowing people who do not follow back), or it works as a browser extension that "clicks" for you. Each of the three ends in a restriction sooner or later, and sometimes in a stolen account.

Checking takes a minute: look the tool up in the platform's list of approved partners. If it is not there, the risk is yours.

## How long limits last

The rough durations we observe in practice: action blocks fall away in 24 to 72 hours, up to two weeks on repeat. Messaging limits: a few days to a week. Suspended live: a week to 30 days depending on severity. Advertising restrictions have no automatic expiry; they wait for a review that takes from a day to several weeks.

More than a month with no change usually means the limit is no longer a temporary measure but a lasting classification of the account. That case calls for a review of the account's whole history and a structured appeal; if you are there, [describe the case to us](/contact) for a free initial assessment.

## What not to do

- **Do not rotate VPN addresses and devices** to "fool" the system. The result is the opposite: the profile looks even more compromised.
- **Do not delete the account** to start clean. The new profile inherits your device and network, and starts with no accumulated trust.
- **Do not buy "restriction removal" from sellers on Telegram and forums.** No outsider has a lever inside Meta's or TikTok's systems; you are paying for a wait that was going to end anyway, or for outright fraud.
- **Do not send the same appeal repeatedly.** Once, precisely, with evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a restriction count as a violation against the account?

Automatic action blocks usually leave no lasting trace if they do not repeat. Restrictions that follow a rule violation (suspended live, removed posts) are recorded in the account's history and influence future decisions.

### Can I speed up the expiry of an action block?

There is no legitimate way to lift an automatic block early. What you can do: stop the actions completely, remove the automation, and appeal through "Tell us" if the block is clearly wrong. The rest is time.

### Why is my ad account restricted when I have not run ads for months?

Ad account reviews are also triggered retroactively: a change of payment method, a new device, a link to another business portfolio, or tighter scrutiny of your whole business category. Inactivity does not protect you from a review.

### The limit expired but my reach is still low. Is that normal?

Yes, for a while. After a period of restrictions the algorithm restores trust gradually, usually over one to three weeks of normal posting. If reach has not returned after a month, check Account Status following the steps in the shadowban article.
