Short answer: shadowban is the unofficial name for a situation where the platform limits the visibility of your content without telling you. The profile works, you post as usual, but reach collapses because your posts stop appearing in recommendations, search and hashtags. Platforms do not use the word "shadowban", but the limits themselves are real, documented in their own tools, and in most cases reversible.

## Does a shadowban even exist?

For years platforms denied it while creators swore it was real. Today the argument is settled in an interesting way: Instagram added an "Account Status" section in settings that states in black and white whether your content is eligible to be recommended to people who do not follow you. TikTok explains in its rules which content is "ineligible for the For You feed". In other words, the mechanism is official; only the name is not. In our reference it appears under its real names: [demotion and removal from recommendations](/vidove-sanktsii#removed-from-recommendations).

That distinction matters in practice. A shadowban is not a mystical punishment but the result of specific classifications of your content and behaviour. And classifications can be checked and changed.

## The symptoms: what it looks like from inside

The typical picture we see in cases:

- Reach falls sharply, by 60–90% within days, with no change in posting frequency or content type.
- In your insights, the share of reached people who are not followers shrinks toward zero.
- Posts do not appear when searching their hashtag, not even for you from a second account.
- Videos stop entering For You or Explore.
- Stories are watched only by your most active core.

A single weak post means nothing; the algorithm is fickle even without penalties. What is worrying is a sustained collapse across all formats at once, especially when it coincides with a violation notice or a removed post.

## How to check whether you are limited

Before you guess, look where the platform states your status itself:

1. **Instagram**: Settings → Account Status. The section shows whether you have violations and whether your profile and content are eligible to be recommended. If a problem lights up there, that is your answer.
2. **TikTok**: check in your insights what share of views comes from For You. A drop from the usual 70–90% to single digits is the signal. Violation notices sit in Settings → Reports.
3. **Facebook**: pages have a page quality section covering distribution limits.
4. **A test from another account**: search for one of your recent posts by its hashtag from an account that does not follow you. Its absence from the results, while you can see it yourself, confirms the limit.

## The real causes

Visibility limits are triggered by a few typical things:

**Borderline content.** Posts that do not break the rules enough to be removed, but that the platform does not want to recommend: sexualized shots, shocking images, health claims, clickbait. This category is official at both Meta and TikTok.

**Accumulated violations.** Every removed post leaves a trace. A few of them in a short period pull the whole account out of recommendations for weeks.

**Spam behaviour.** Mass following and unfollowing, identical comments, excessive posting, bots and "growth" services. Algorithms recognize them by the pattern, not by the intent.

**Problem hashtags.** Some hashtags are periodically blocked after spam waves. A post with a blocked hashtag loses visibility entirely, and sometimes drags the profile with it.

**Music and copyright.** Repeated use of protected audio or reposted third-party content reduces distribution, especially on TikTok and Reels.

## What actually helps

The steps we apply, and that have an effect:

1. Open Account Status and appeal every violation you believe is wrong. A successful appeal clears the trace, not just the individual post.
2. Remove or archive the content on the edge: posts with blocked hashtags, borderline shots, reposted third-party videos.
3. Stop every automation and "growth service", and change the password to cut off third-party app access.
4. Slow down for a week: post less often, but original content, with no recycled formats.
5. Track the share of non-followers in your insights. Recovery usually shows up there first, within two to four weeks.

## The differences between platforms

The mechanism is similar everywhere, but the details diverge enough to confuse the diagnosis.

**Instagram** limits on two levels: individual posts (excluded from Explore and hashtag pages) and the whole account (excluded from recommendations to non-followers). The second is visible in Account Status, the first is not always; so check both.

**TikTok** works post by post more than the rest. One video can be "under limit" and shown only to your followers, while the next one takes off in For You again. A prolonged collapse of the whole account there almost always means accumulated violations in Reports or detected automation.

**Facebook** applies limits mainly to pages and groups: reduced distribution after reports of false news or clickbait, and since 2025 more aggressively against unoriginal content. The page quality section shows the accumulated problems.

**YouTube** has no classic shadowban, but it does have demonetization and exclusion from recommendations for borderline content; the diagnostic logic is the same: check the status in Studio first, guess later.

## How long recovery takes: the realistic curve

In the cases we have run, reach almost never comes back as a jump — it comes back as a staircase. Nothing changes in the first week after the cause is cleared, and this is exactly where most people give up or start "fixing" things that work. In the second week, non-followers in the insights move off zero. A full return to previous levels takes three to six weeks of consistent posting, and longer for accounts with a heavy violation history. Plan with that horizon and measure the trend, not the individual post.

## The myths that waste time

**"A 48-hour break lifts a shadowban."** A break helps only if the cause was spam-level posting. With borderline content or accumulated violations, silence clears nothing.

**"Run an ad and the algorithm forgives you."** Paid distribution runs on a different system. The ad will reach the audience, but the organic limit stays.

**"Delete the whole account and start clean."** You lose years of content and trust over a problem that is usually solvable. And a new account with no history starts with less algorithmic trust, not more.

One distinction at the end: a shadowban is a silent limit on visibility. If you received an explicit notice that a feature has been switched off (comments, live, messages), that is an official restriction and follows a different path; we devoted a [separate article](/blog/ogranicheniya-restriktsii-na-akaunta-vidove-i-reshenia) to it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a shadowban last?

With the cause cleared: usually between a few days and four weeks. Without removing the cause, the limit renews indefinitely, so the order is diagnosis first, waiting second.

### Can a new account be shadowbanned?

Yes, and more easily. New accounts have no accumulated trust, and an aggressive start (mass following, many posts a day, external links everywhere) puts them into a spam classification within days.

### Does deleting old posts help?

Selectively yes: removing specific posts with blocked hashtags or borderline content is part of the fix. Deleting dozens of posts at once, though, looks like spam behaviour in itself; do it in batches.

### Instagram says everything is fine, but my reach is still low. Why?

If Account Status is clean, this is more likely an algorithmic dip: a shift in your audience's interests, a format performing worse, or seasonality. That is not a shadowban and it is solved with content, not appeals.
